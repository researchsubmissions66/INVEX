Invariance & Expressiveness
Core Dimensions

The Key Idea

Evaluating pathology foundation models purely intrinsically, without labels or fine-tuning.

Central Hypothesis

  • Robust to Variations: A strong pathology representation must handle variations that don't change the underlying tissue morphology.
  • Arbitrary Orientations: Histopathology images inherently exhibit arbitrary orientations due to tissue prep and scanning, but their biological semantics remain unchanged.
  • Consistent Embeddings: An effective model should produce consistent (invariant) embeddings for rotated views of the exact same tissue.
  • Discriminative Information: While being rotation-invariant, the model must maintain sufficient expressiveness (discriminative power) to successfully distinguish different biological structures.
Invariance x Expressiveness
0° Rotation
Invariant Representation

Rotating a tissue patch does not alter its underlying morphology or diagnostic content. Consequently, an ideal pathology representation should assign nearly identical embeddings to rotated versions of the same tissue patch.

Invariance x Expressiveness
Collapsed (Poor Expressiveness)
Separated (High Expressiveness)

Expressiveness ensures the model does not collapse all data points together. It must preserve enough discriminative information to clearly separate different biological structures across the manifold.
